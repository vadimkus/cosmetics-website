## The mango mystery, solved

Yesterday's audit of the serum flagged that its deck listed "Mango seed butter"
in the antioxidant column when no mango material of any kind was in the serum
formula.

**Mango seed butter is real, and it is in this product at 0.800%.**
`Mangifera Indica Seed Butter`, a functional dose for an emollient butter, and the
safety assessment notes the CIR maximum reported use is 5% in leave-on dermal
products, so 0.8% sits comfortably inside it.

So the serum's deck slide was copy-pasted from the cream's. The serum error
stands as recorded; the cream claim is sound.

## The two products are genuinely different, and the certificates prove it

This is not the serum in a jar. It is a different formula built for a different
job, and the numbers make that unusually clear.

| | Serum (22) | Cream (32) |
|---|---|---|
| **Glycerin** | **25.45%** | **8.00%** |
| Emollients and oils | ~2.4% | **~13.0%** |
| Niacinamide | 2.00% (assayed 96.72%) | 2.00% (assayed **101.30%**) |
| Adenosine | 0.04% (assayed 101.00%) | 0.04% (assayed **95.50%**) |
| Bakuchiol | 0.100% | 0.100% |
| Allantoin | 0.100% | 0.100% |
| **Peptides** | six, ~1.4 ppm | **none at all** |
| **Mango seed butter** | none | **0.800%** |
| Lavender oil | 0.0186% | **0.0413%** |
| Declared allergens | linalool | linalool **+ limonene** |
| pH | 6.78 | 6.23 |
| **Specific gravity** | **1.0689** | **0.9860** |

**The specific gravity is the elegant part.** The serum weighs *more* than water
because a quarter of it is glycerin. The cream weighs *less* than water because
it is loaded with oils — ethylhexyl palmitate 4%, cetearyl alcohol 3%,
caprylic/capric triglyceride 2.4%, butylene glycol dicaprylate 2%, dimethicone
0.8%, mango butter 0.8%. Two certificates, two numbers either side of 1.000, and
they are a physical fingerprint of the formulation difference.

Which gives the honest routine argument: **the serum pulls water in, the cream
holds it there.** The serum's own how-to already says "follow with a
moisturiser". This is that moisturiser. That is a reason to own both which does
not require inventing anything.

## The cream is arguably the more honest of the pair

It has **no peptides**. The serum carries six premium peptides at 0.05–1.1 ppm
and sells them as six mechanisms. The cream simply does not have them, so there
is nothing to over-claim.

What remains at trace here is smaller: the ceramide / cholesterol /
phytosphingosine "lipid barrier liposome" at 0.1 ppm each, hydrolyzed collagen at
0.1 ppm, hydrolyzed elastin at 1 ppm and propolis extract at 10 ppm. Deck slides
8 and 9 attribute barrier binding and dermal structure to those, which is not
deliverable at a tenth of a part per million.

**What is at a working dose:** glycerin 8.00%, niacinamide 2.00%, mango seed
butter 0.800%, dimethicone 0.800%, hydroxyacetophenone 0.500%, allantoin 0.100%,
bakuchiol 0.100%, adenosine 0.040%, plus roughly 13% of emollients and structure.

## Bakuchiol, same figure, same caveat

**0.100%**, exactly as in the serum. Deck slide 7 repeats the retinol comparison
verbatim, and the study behind it — Dhaliwal et al., *British Journal of
Dermatology* 2019;180(2):289–296 — used **0.5% twice daily**, five times this
concentration. Verified from the journal yesterday.

Neither COA assays bakuchiol. Niacinamide and adenosine are assayed on both
products; the headline ingredient is not assayed on either.

## Korea licenses it for two things, not three

Deck slide 2 states the reformulation kept the "dual-functionality (anti-wrinkle
and skin brightening)" of the product it replaced. There is no UV filter in the
formula, so unlike the BB products (41, 42) and the sunscreens (39, 40) this is a
**dual-function** cosmetic: wrinkle improvement via adenosine 0.04%, brightening
via niacinamide 2.00%.

## Deck slide 2 is a reformulation record worth keeping

It documents what this product replaced:

- **Before — "INTENSIVE MULTI FUNCTIONAL CREAM":** phytosphingosine,
  Lactobacillus/pumpkin ferment extract, and the Phaseolus radiatus / betula bark
  / Rumex crispus trio (Phytolex), plus adenosine and niacinamide.
- **After — this product:** bakuchiol, propolis extract, phytosphingosine,
  hydrolyzed collagen, hydrolyzed elastin, adenosine, niacinamide.

So they dropped the pumpkin ferment and the Phytolex trio and brought in
bakuchiol, propolis, collagen and elastin, keeping the texture, fragrance and
dual function. Useful context for anyone who used the old jar.

## What the safety assessment supports

- Conclusion: "The product is considered safe with restrictions for human health
  when used under normal or reasonably foreseeable conditions of use."
- **Patch test graded "Non Irritant" by Dr Koziej**, with the assessor clearing
  "dermatologically tested" for the label and noting the volunteer count is not
  statistically significant. Same as the serum.
- Mango seed butter checked against a CIR maximum reported use of 5% in leave-on
  products; this formula uses 0.8%.

## It is more scented than the serum

Lavender oil at **0.0413%** against the serum's 0.0186%, with **two** declared
allergens rather than one: linalool at 0.0266% and **limonene at 0.0021%**.
Nothing on the site mentions either. Anyone who tolerated the serum on fragrance
grounds should know the cream carries more of it.
