# Post-market: CERABARRIER BIOME GEL CLEANSER — mucosal irritation case — 2026-07-02

**Product:** GENOSYS CERABARRIER BIOME GEL CLEANSER (200ml GCCL05 / 600ml GCCL06)
**LOT:** 6682FD (mfg 2026-04-24, COA pH 6.37)
**Source formula:** Intertek Formula PDF + Formula.xlsx (verified, 37 ingredients) — see [SESSION_CHANGES_2026-07-02_CERRABAR_FOLDER_INGEST.md](./SESSION_CHANGES_2026-07-02_CERRABAR_FOLDER_INGEST.md)
**Reported event date:** 2026-07-02
**Reporter:** brand owner (verbal, no PII recorded)
**Severity:** non-serious (localised irritation, no medical treatment confirmed at time of log)
**No personal/identifying data stored — formulation analysis only.**

## Report

User applied the cleanser to the glans penis (mucous membrane / mucocutaneous tissue) — **off-label use**. Face use produced no reaction. Genital use produced **strong irritation of the glans**. Product noted as "very liquid / runny" by reporter.

## Label warning already present

Artwork (EN/KR/DE/FR/TR/RU/AR) states: *"For external use only. Avoid contact with eyes and mucous membranes."* Genital mucosal use is therefore outside the labelled directions. **On-pack "DERMATOLOGICALLY TESTED" covers keratinized skin only — mucosal use was not tested.**

## Root-cause analysis (formulation)

Trace actives (5 ceramides, Lactobacillus/Bifida ferments, Epilobium, Chicory, Dandelion, Anastatica, Phytosphingosine, Cholesterol, Shea, Hydrogenated Lecithin) are all at ≤0.0001% (≤1 ppm) — **cannot be the cause**. Irritation driven by the surfactant system + fragrance:

| Component | % W/W | Role in mucosal irritation |
|---|---:|---|
| Cocamidopropyl Betaine | 6.0 | **Prime suspect.** Documented allergen (ACDS Allergen of the Year 2004); common cause of allergic/irritant contact dermatitis from cosmetics; impurities DMAPA / amidoamine sensitize. On mucosa: irritant + sensitizer. |
| Sodium Cocoyl Glutamate | 8.75 | Anionic surfactant; strips mucosal lipids; penetration high on non-keratinized tissue. |
| Decyl Glucoside | 1.65 | Nonionic surfactant; adds to total surfactant load. |
| Parfum (Fragrance) | 0.5 | Mucosal irritant; possible sensitizer. |
| 1,2-Hexanediol | 0.35 | Solvent / preservative; mucosal stinging possible. |
| Ethylhexylglycerin | 0.35 | Preservative synergist; mild mucosal irritant. |
| Citric Acid | 0.4 | pH adjuster (finished pH 6.37). |

**Total surfactant load ~16.4%** (8.75 + 6.0 + 1.65) — normal for facial rinse-off, high for mucosal contact.

## Why face OK, glans not

1. **Anatomy:** face = keratinized stratified squamous epithelium with stratum corneum + sebum. Glans = mucocutaneous / partially non-keratinized mucosa, thin barrier, no sebum, highly vascularized, high permeability.
2. **Foreskin trapping:** if uncircumcised, surfactant migrates under the prepuce and is retained in the inner preputial mucosa — contact time multiplied. Classic mechanism for soap-induced balanitis-type irritation.
3. **Contact time + friction + warm water:** genital washing typically longer and more mechanical than facial cleansing.
4. **"Very liquid" consistency:** COA spec is "watery viscous liquid" (in spec, not a defect), but low viscosity (thickener = Sodium Chloride 1% only) increases run-off onto mucosa, harder to dose, longer smear time before rinse.

## Diagnosis category (formulation-level, not medical)

Most likely **irritant contact dermatitis** from surfactant + fragrance on mucosa in off-label use. Less likely but possible: **allergic contact dermatitis to Cocamidopropyl Betaine** (would recur on any CAPB-containing product). Final classification is clinical.

## Product verdict

**Not defective, not dangerous when used as directed.** COA passed, pH in spec, microbiology clean, labelling correct and already warns against mucosal contact. Adverse event is attributable to off-label application on mucous membrane.

## Brand actions

1. **Log this case** in the cosmetic post-market surveillance file (this document). Monitor for repeat reports.
2. **Customer guidance:** rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water, discontinue intimate/mucosal use; if irritation persists >1–3 days or worsens, see a doctor (dermatologist/urologist) to differentiate irritant vs allergic. Emollient + oral antihistamine typically suffice for irritant form — clinical decision, not brand guidance.
3. **Website / product card:** add explicit RU + AR line: «Только для наружного применения на коже лица. Не применять на слизистых и в интимных зонах.» / «للاستخدام الخارجي على بشرة الوجه فقط. لا يستخدم على الأغشية المخاطية والمناطق الحساسة.» On-pack EN warning already exists.
4. **Raise with DTS MG (manufacturer):**
   - (a) Review Cocamidopropyl Betaine 6% and Parfum 0.5% vs the "barrier / microbiome / sensitive" claim positioning — CAPB + fragrance partly contradict the sensitive-skin narrative.
   - (b) Increase viscosity (beyond NaCl 1% salt thickening) to reduce run-off onto mucosa — UX + safety margin.
   - (c) Consider a fragrance-free / CAPB-free variant for an intimate-safe SKU if the line is intended for body use.

## Regulatory note

Under UAE/TDRA + EU cosmetic regs, non-serious undesirable effects are recorded in the cosmetic post-market file (PIF/CPSR adjacency). If any case meets "serious" criteria (hospitalisation, permanent impairment, congenital anomaly), escalation/reporting thresholds apply. This case = non-serious as of log date.

## UPDATE — 2026-07-02 (same day, post-treatment response)

**New clinical information:** the affected user applied **Canesten (clotrimazole 1%, topical antifungal)** and reported **substantial improvement within 2–3 days** — itching resolving, glans color restoring, redness subsiding.

**Revised diagnosis:** **Candida balanitis (fungal)** is now the leading diagnosis, NOT irritant contact dermatitis. A rapid, substantial response to a topical antifungal is the expected course for Candida balanitis and would not occur for pure irritant/allergic contact dermatitis. Treatment response is a stronger diagnostic signal than the initial onset history. (Formal confirmation = clinical exam ± KOH microscopy/culture; this log reflects response-to-treat, a valid practical signal.)

**Revised role of the cleanser:** the product is **not a direct toxic/caustic agent**. It is best characterised as a **predisposing co-factor**: the surfactant system (Cocamidopropyl Betaine 6% + Sodium Cocoyl Glutamate 8.75% + Parfum 0.5%) disrupted the mucosal lipid barrier and commensal flora on the glans / under the prepuce, and the low-viscosity run-off left residual surfactant in a moist, occluded environment — the conditions under which opportunistic Candida overgrows into symptomatic balanitis. This is a recognised mechanism for soap/cleanser-triggered balanitis, especially in uncircumcised men in hot/humid climates (UAE). The cleanser "opened the door" to a fungus that was likely already present at low level; it did not directly burn the mucosa.

**Revised product verdict:** unchanged in substance — **not defective, not dangerous when used as directed (keratinized facial skin)**. The direct-toxicity safety signal is weaker, but the **use-area signal is stronger**: the cleanser can act as a trigger for candidal balanitis in predisposed users via mucosal barrier/microbiome disruption. This reinforces (does not weaken) the need for explicit "not for mucosal/intimate use" copy in RU and AR on the website product card and marketing.

**Irony noted for brand:** the product's "microbiome / barrier" claim positioning is contradicted on mucosal tissue, where its surfactant system disrupts exactly the barrier and microbiome it claims to support. Fine for facial keratinized skin; not appropriate for genital mucosa.

**Customer guidance (updated):**
1. Complete the full Canesten course (do not stop when symptoms clear — standard balanitis regimen ~2x/day for 7–14 days, several days beyond symptom resolution; per leaflet/pharmacist). Stopping early → recurrence.
2. If uncircumcised: retract foreskin, apply under prepuce, keep the area **dry** after washing (Candida thrives in moisture).
3. No further gels/soaps in the intimate area — water or a dedicated mild intimate product only. CERABARRIER is not for this use.
4. If recurrence, severe without clear trigger, or other risk factors → doctor: check blood glucose (recurrent candidal balanitis in a man warrants excluding diabetes), consider oral fluconazole, partner treatment, screen for other causes.

**DTS MG formulation discussion (retained, lower urgency):** CAPB %, fragrance load, and viscosity remain worth reviewing against the "barrier/sensitive" positioning, but the direct-safety urgency is reduced given the fungal etiology.

**Brand actions (unchanged, reinforced):** add explicit RU + AR "external use on facial skin only / not for mucous membranes or intimate areas" copy to the product card and marketing. Keep this case in the PMS file as a use-area trigger event, not a product defect.
