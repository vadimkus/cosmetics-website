## The thing nobody mentions

**Glycerin at 25.45%.** A quarter of the bottle. It is the second ingredient
after water, it is sourced as a branded grade (ELOGLYN R995, LG H&H) at 25.0%
with a further 0.22% arriving inside the emulsifier, and the specific gravity on
the certificate — 1.0689 against a 1.050–1.070 spec — is exactly what a
quarter-glycerin serum weighs.

Nothing on genosys.ae mentions glycerin at all. The description leads instead
with a "peptide complex" that is present at roughly **1.4 parts per million**.

This is a serious humectant serum. That is not a criticism — 25% glycerin in a
30 ml bottle is a real formulation decision that will visibly plump and hold
water in Gulf air. It is simply not what the product is being sold on.

## Bakuchiol at 0.1%, and the study behind the retinol claim

The whole product is positioned on bakuchiol as "a natural alternative to
retinol". Deck slide 6 states that "bakuchiol and retinol both significantly
decreased wrinkle surface area with no statistical difference between the
compounds."

That sentence is a faithful summary of a real, good study. **Dhaliwal et al.,
British Journal of Dermatology 2019;180(2):289–296** — randomised, double-blind,
12 weeks, 44 patients. Verified from the journal, not from memory.

**The study used bakuchiol at 0.5%, applied twice daily.** This serum contains
**0.100%** — one fifth of the studied concentration, in a once- or twice-daily
serum.

So the ingredient is genuinely present at a number that reads like a dose, and
the mechanism claims are real, but the specific head-to-head-with-retinol
evidence sits at 5× this concentration. The page states the 0.1%, states the
study's 0.5%, and lets the reader do the arithmetic. That is more useful than
either repeating the claim or dropping bakuchiol entirely.

Also worth noting: the COA assays niacinamide and adenosine but **does not assay
bakuchiol**, so there is no measured figure for the headline ingredient.

## The peptides: excellent sourcing, homeopathic dosing

The safety assessment's trade-name table is where this becomes clear. "Anti-aging
Peptide 6" is not one branded complex — it is **four separate premium raw
materials, each bought in at 0.1% of the formula**:

| Raw material | Supplier | In formula | Peptide it carries | In finished product |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MATRIXYL 3000** | Sederma SAS | 0.10% | Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 | **0.1 ppm** |
| | | | Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 | **0.05–0.08 ppm** |
| **SYN-COLL** | DSM | 0.10% | Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5 | **1.1 ppm** |
| **AH PEP 50(H)** | Danjoungbio | 0.10% | Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 | **0.05 ppm** |
| **Elastyl™ MB** | CORUM | 0.10% | Palmitoyl Hexapeptide-12 | **0.1 ppm** |

Plus Dipeptide-2 at 0.1 ppm. **Six peptides, roughly 1.4 ppm combined.**

Give credit where it is due: Matrixyl 3000, Syn-Coll and Elastyl are respected,
expensive materials from Sederma, DSM and Corum. Somebody chose well. They are
simply present at a fraction of the level these suppliers exist to sell.

Deck slide 7 attributes a specific mechanism to each of the six — collagen
stimulation, inflammation reduction, glycosaminoglycan synthesis, facial muscle
relaxation. At 0.05 to 1.1 ppm, none of that is deliverable. Name the peptides;
attach nothing to them.

## The clinical study we cite and do not hold

The site description currently states:

> Clinical study on improvement of skin age index, P&K Skin Research Center,
> Feb. 22 to May 13, 2024, 24 adult women aged 30~59 years.

That citation is unusually specific — a named Korean CRO, exact dates, an n and
an age band — which suggests it is real and that DTS MG has the report.

**We do not have it.** A search of the entire `~/Desktop/Drive/Genosys` tree
returns no P&K report, no skin-age-index study and no 2024 efficacy report for
this product. The 46-page safety assessment does not reference it either.

Consequently **no result from that study appears on the new page**, and the
citation has been removed from the record rather than left as an unsupported
half-claim. This is the single highest-value thing to chase: a real 24-subject
clinical study on skin age index would be the strongest asset this product has,
and it is currently doing nothing for us because nobody can see it.

## What the safety assessment does support

  human health when used under normal or reasonably foreseeable conditions of
  use."

  "Non Irritant"**, with the assessor clearing the "dermatologically tested"
  claim for the label. Same caveat as on products 39 and 22's siblings: the
  assessor notes the volunteer count is not statistically significant.

"Non Irritant" is a stronger result than a bare pass and is worth saying.

## It is scented, and that was never said

Lavender oil at 0.0186% with **linalool at 0.0114% declared separately as an
allergen**. It is a natural essential oil rather than a synthetic perfume, but
for anyone screening for fragrance or for linalool specifically, it is there and
the site has never mentioned it.

## Errors in our own material

For `~/Desktop/genosys-artwork-corrections.html`:

1. **The cited P&K clinical study report is not in our possession.** Removed
   from the record; request it from DTS MG.
2. **Deck slide 3 lists "Mango seed butter"** in the antioxidant column. There is
   **no mango seed butter in the formula** — no Mangifera or Irvingia material of
   any kind. A DTS MG deck error.
3. **Deck slide 5 claims bakuchiol is a "safe plant derived ingredient without
   side effects"** — an absolute safety claim no cosmetic ingredient can carry —
   and that "it prevents acne with excellent anti-inflammatory and antibacterial
   properties", which is a drug claim. Neither may reach any GENOSYS UAE material.
4. **Nothing on the site mentions glycerin at 25.45%**, the largest active
   ingredient by an order of magnitude.
5. **Nothing mentions the lavender oil or the declared linalool.**
6. **Six peptides and the lipid liposome are sold as mechanisms** at 0.05 to
   1.1 ppm.
