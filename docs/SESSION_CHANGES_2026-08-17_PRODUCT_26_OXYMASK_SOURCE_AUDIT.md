# Product 26 — EGF REPAIR OXYMASK CREAM — source audit

The last cream in the range, and the one where the manufacturer has already done
the honest thing for us.

## The carton declares the trace dose itself

The Korean panel of the registered artwork prints:

> 에스에이치-올리고펩타이드-1 **(0.1ppm)**

**sh-Oligopeptide-1 — the EGF the product is named after — with "0.1 ppm"
printed next to it on the box.** No other GENOSYS carton audited this week
declares a concentration inside the ingredient list.

That changes the tone of the whole page. Every other product this week required
us to be the first to say a headline ingredient was at trace. Here the
manufacturer says it on pack, so stating it plainly is matching the carton rather
than contradicting the marketing. It is also a genuine trust signal worth showing.

## What actually makes this product work

Our record names six key ingredients. **Neither of the two doing the work is among
them.**

A perfluorocarbon at 5% plus a sugar surfactant at 2.75% **is** the oxymask. The
bubbling everyone buys it for is those two ingredients, and our copy credited it
to EGF instead.

## The named ingredients, at their real concentrations

| Our record calls it a key ingredient | Actual |
|---|---|
| sh-Oligopeptide-1 (EGF) | **0.1 ppm** — and the carton says so |
| Madecassoside | **1 ppm** |
| Copper Tripeptide-1 | **0.05 ppm** = 50 parts per billion |
| SEPITONIC M3 (mineral complex) | **~10 ppm** total |
| Salmon Oil | **100 ppm** |
| Adenosine | **0.040%** — the one that is real |

For scale: the madecassoside here is **200 times lower** than the centella
triterpenes in the Soothing Repair Postcream, and the copper tripeptide-1 is
**1,000 times lower** than in the ND Cell cream.

**SEPITONIC M3** is a SEPPIC S.A. trade name, mapped only in the safety
assessment: magnesium aspartate 4.75% + zinc gluconate 4.75% + copper gluconate
0.5% + phenoxyethanol 1.0%, used at 0.0100% of the formula. The Russian carton
panel independently confirms the same three minerals.

**A nice consequence:** the phenoxyethanol on the ingredient list is at **1 ppm**
and arrives *inside* Sepitonic M3. It is a carryover from a mineral premix, not an
added preservative. Preservation is actually the glycols — 1,2-hexanediol at 2%
with ethylhexylglycerin.

## Salmon oil, and the vegan question

**Salmon Oil at 0.0100% — 100 ppm.** Supplier trade name "Bouncell", 100% salmon
oil, so the finished figure is straightforward.

It makes the product **not vegan**, and it is fish-derived, which matters to
anyone avoiding fish products. Our record names it as a key ingredient without
either disclosure. Same class of omission as the peanut oil on product 23.

## The usage instructions are the most valuable thing we were not passing on

The carton and the deck agree, and none of it is on our site:

1. **Apply to dry skin.** Not damp, dry.
2. **Do not rub it in.** Spread it and wait for the bubbles to cover the face.
3. **Pump 3–5 times** for a dose (Korean panel).
4. When the bubbles start popping, gently massage and tap.
5. **Do not rinse off** — despite being called a mask.
6. Morning and evening.

For a product whose entire mechanism is a foaming reaction, "apply to dry skin and
do not rub" is the difference between it working and not working. That belongs at
the top of the how-to, not in a dossier.

## The clinical study exists, but we hold no number

Checked the deck per the standing rule, and it is worth doing carefully here.

The Intertek assessment records **"Other Tests: None presented"** and the phrase
"external stimulus" appears nowhere in it. So on the dossier alone our claim of an
*"Efficacy test on skin soothing effect against external stimulus"* looks
unsupported.

The deck has a **CLINICAL TRIAL DATA** page positioned immediately after this
product's ingredient pages, and it is titled:

> **Clinical study on skin soothing effect against external stimulus (physical
> stimulus)**

So the study is real and the title matches our claim exactly. **But unlike the
other products in the same deck, this page carries no extractable figure** — the
Problem Control Serum page gives "reduces sebum by 17%", the Intensive Hydro
Soothing page gives "+12% hydration, −1 °C", and this one gives only the title,
with the result presumably in a chart image.

So the honest position, and what the page says: a clinical study with that exact
title was conducted, we hold no result figure for it, and we have asked for the
report. That is different from both "unsupported" and "proven".

## Korea licenses it for wrinkles, on adenosine

The Korean panel reads `[주름개선 기능성화장품]` — a wrinkle-improvement functional
cosmetic, single function, `주성분: 아데노신` (main ingredient adenosine). The
English panel's "skin nourishing, skin regenerating" is marketing wording, not a
licensed functional claim.

Correspondingly the COA runs **two** adenosine tests: an identity check against a
standard chromatogram, and an assay returning **0.043%** against a declared
0.040% with a >90% specification.

## What the assessment supports

- Conclusion: **"safe for human health"**, with no "with restrictions" qualifier.
- **Patch test graded "Non Irritant" by QACS Ltd**, clearing "dermatologically
  tested".
- **Limonene at 0.0016%** declared, arising from the eucalyptus oil, which is above
  the 0.001% threshold. No parfum in the formula.
- An open note on **eucalyptol**: the Council of Europe committee considered the
  toxicological data "too limited and not of an optimal quality" to set a TDI. The
  assessor still concluded the product safe. Worth knowing internally; not
  customer copy.

## Errors and gaps for `~/Desktop/genosys-artwork-corrections.html`

1. **★ The usage instructions were missing from our site entirely** — dry skin, do
   not rub, 3–5 pumps, do not rinse. On a product that works by foaming, this is
   functional information, not nice-to-have.
2. **★ Request the clinical report.** The deck names the study but shows no
   figure, so we can reference the study and nothing more.
3. **Salmon oil was undisclosed** as an animal-derived and fish-derived ingredient.
4. **Pregnancy and lactation avoidance** was on the carton and not on our site.
5. **The Russian panel says "Объем: 50 г"** — "volume" with a mass unit. Milder
   than the four cartons already logged, which paired "Объем" with millilitres on
   weight products, but the same wording family. Add it to the Russian panel audit
   request rather than treating it as separate.
6. **The deck's EGF claims must never reach our material:** it credits sh-
   Oligopeptide-1 with stimulating keratinocyte proliferation and with wound
   healing, "at the inflammation stage EGF attracts cells to the wound site". At
   **0.1 ppm**, and these are drug claims.
7. **No period-after-opening symbol** appears in the artwork, unlike products 25
   and 28 which both carry 6M. Worth asking whether one is required, since this is
   a pump pack that stays open for months.
