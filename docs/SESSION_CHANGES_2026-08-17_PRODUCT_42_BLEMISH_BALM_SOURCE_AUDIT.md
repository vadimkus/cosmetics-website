completes a genuinely surprising pattern across the range.

## The pattern this product completes

| Product | Filters | Filter load | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39 Ultra Shield | 6 | 17.10% | **SPF50+ / PA++++** |
| 40 Multi Sun | 4 | 18.50% | SPF40 / PA++ |
| **42 Blemish Balm Cream** | **3** | **19.70%** | **SPF30 / PA++** |

**Perfectly inverted.** Every step down the range adds filter by weight and
loses grade. This one carries the most filter of the three and rates lowest.

Two reasons, and both are honest:

1. **No long-UVA filter.** Titanium dioxide reaches short UVA and stops;
   octinoxate and octocrylene are UVB absorbers, with octocrylene contributing a
   little short UVA and mainly acting as a photostabiliser for the octinoxate.
   Only product 39 carries dedicated long-UVA filters.
2. **The titanium dioxide here is doing two jobs at once.** At 7.70% it is both
   the largest UV filter and the white pigment that gives the cream its
   coverage. Pigment-grade titanium dioxide dispersed for opacity is not fully
   optically available as a UV filter, so you cannot read 7.70% as 7.70% of
   protection. That is exactly why a tinted base with 19.7% filter lands at
   SPF30.

## The carton carries a warning our site does not

Korean precaution 4 on the registered artwork:

> 동일 성분(알부틴 2% 이상)을 함유하는 제품의 「인체적용 시험자료」에서
> 구진과 경미한 가려움이 보고된 예가 있음

"In human application test data for products containing the same ingredient
(**arbutin at 2% or more**), there have been reported cases of **papules and
mild itching**."

This is a **mandatory Korean disclosure triggered by the arbutin level**, it is
printed on the box the customer receives, and it appears nowhere on
genosys.ae. Meanwhile our record claims the product is "especially" suitable for
"sensitive and post-treatment skin".

It goes on the page. Not buried — arbutin at 2% is the reason the product can
make a brightening claim at all, so the tradeoff belongs next to the benefit.

## Korea licenses it for three things

`[미백 . 주름개선 . 자외선차단 삼중 기능성화장품]` — a triple-function cosmetic
for **whitening, wrinkle improvement and UV protection**, with the declared
actives listed as titanium dioxide, ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate, octocrylene,
**arbutin** and **adenosine**.

Note the difference from the BB Cushion (41), which earns its brightening
function on **niacinamide 2%**. This one uses **arbutin 2%**. Same claim, two
different actives, and only one of them carries a mandatory itching warning.

Two things worth drawing out.

**The hydroquinone test is the standout.** Arbutin hydrolyses to hydroquinone,
which is banned in cosmetics. A brand selling arbutin at 2% and testing the
finished product for hydroquinone degradation — and publishing under 1 ppm — is
doing the thing that actually matters. Almost nobody shows this.

**Arbutin measured 1.81% against 2.00% declared.** That is 90.5% of label, and
the specification is "more than 90%", so it passes — but only just. The page
should print the measured figure alongside the declared one, as product 40's
page does, rather than only the round number.

Lead and arsenic testing matters here specifically because the shade system is
iron oxides.

## Five things it genuinely does not contain

The carton prints a "5 No-additions" mark: **paraben, artificial fragrance,
mineral oil, ethanol, phenoxyethanol.** All five check out against the formula —
preservation is caprylyl glycol, glyceryl caprylate, caprylhydroxamic acid,
tropolone and 1,2-hexanediol.

**This is the only fragrance-free product of the three SPF items.** Multi Sun
(40) is fragranced at 0.25% with five declared allergens; Ultra Shield (39)
carries parfum at 0.5%. For reactive skin this is the one to reach for on that
axis — which sits in interesting tension with the arbutin warning, and the page
should present both rather than pick the flattering one.

## The silicones, and a date worth knowing

**Cyclopentasiloxane (D5) at 3.50% and cyclohexasiloxane (D6) at 2.50%** — 6%
combined. They are what make a heavily pigmented, 19.7%-filter cream spread like
a light base instead of a paste.

Verified regulatory position, from the source:

- **Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/1328** (16 May 2024) amends REACH Annex XVII
  entry 70. D4, D5 and D6 may not be placed on the market in mixtures at
  **0.1% or more** — from **6 June 2026** generally, and by derogation **from
  6 June 2027 for leave-on cosmetics**.
- The grounds are **environmental, not skin safety**. D4/D5/D6 were identified
  as substances of very high concern with very persistent and very
  bioaccumulative properties, and RAC concluded that leave-on cosmetics are the
  main source of releases.

So: this is a REACH restriction on the EU market, the product is sold in the
UAE, and nothing here is non-compliant where it is sold. But **this formula
cannot be placed on the EU market after 6 June 2027 without reformulation**, and
that is worth telling Vadim independently of the page.

## Also true, and not currently said anywhere

- **One shade only.** The formula has a single iron-oxide shade system. The BB
  Cushion (41) ships in three (Ivory, Beige, Camel). A one-shade base is a real
  limitation for a UAE customer base and should be stated rather than discovered.
- **Contains beeswax at 2%** — not vegan. Nothing on the site mentions it.
- **No water-resistance test**, and no claim on the carton.
- pH 7.44 is high for skincare and near the top of its own range, which is
  normal for a mineral-loaded tinted base.
- Manufactured by WINNOVA Co., Ltd — **contract manufacturer, do not name.**
