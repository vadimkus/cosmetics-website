## The filters, and the interesting part

| Filter | % | Covers | EU Annex VI cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethylhexyl Methoxycinnamate** (octinoxate) | **7.50** | UVB | 10% (entry 12) |
| **Ethylhexyl Salicylate** (octisalate) | **5.00** | UVB | **5% (entry 20)** |
| **Titanium Dioxide** | **3.00** | UVB + short UVA | 25% (entry 27) |
| **Isoamyl p-Methoxycinnamate** (amiloxate) | **3.00** | UVB | 10% (entry 14) |

**Total filter load: 18.50%.**

That is **more filter by weight than Ultra Shield's 17.10%**, and it produces a
*lower* grade: SPF40 / PA++ against SPF50+ / PA++++. That is not a defect and
it is the most interesting thing on the page — three of the four filters here
are UVB absorbers, and the only UVA cover is titanium dioxide, which reaches
short UVA and stops. There is no dedicated long-UVA filter of the kind Ultra
Shield gets from Tinosorb S and Mexoryl-type TDSA. Hence PA++, moderate, rather
than PA++++.

So the honest positioning is: **this is the everyday one**, and the difference
between the two is UVA cover, not effort.

Octisalate sits at **exactly the 5% legal ceiling**, which is worth knowing but
is not a problem — it is a cap, and the formula is at it, not over it.

Limits verified against the consolidated Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009, Annex VI,
text as at 1 May 2026 (EUR-Lex CELEX 02009R1223-20260501).

## Octinoxate: this needs saying out loud

**This product contains octinoxate at 7.50%.** Product 39's page — which I built
yesterday and which links to this one as the lighter option — carries the claim
"no oxybenzone, no octinoxate", and that claim is true of 39. A customer moving
from that page to this one would reasonably assume the pair share the property.
They do not.

The regulatory position, verified from the source rather than assumed:

- **SCCS/1671/24**, adopted 26 June 2025 with a corrigendum of 26 March 2026,
  concludes that ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate **is safe as a UV filter up to 10%**
  in sunscreen lotion, face and hand cream and lipstick, used separately or in
  combination. It also states the products are safe for children, on margin of
  safety grounds.
- The same opinion **confirms EHMC is endocrine-active** — estrogenic and weakly
  anti-androgenic, in vitro and in vivo. That was not withdrawn; it was managed
  through exposure modelling.
- The SCCS states explicitly that **its mandate did not cover environmental
  safety**. That is precisely the reef question, so nobody should read "SCCS says
  safe" as an answer to it.
- Annex VI entry 12 permits 10%. This formula uses 7.50%.

The page should state the concentration, the cap, the endocrine finding and the
environmental gap, and then let the reader decide. It should also say plainly
that if avoiding octinoxate is the point, product 39 is the one to buy. That is
the rare case where the honest thing and the commercial thing agree — 39 is the
more expensive product.

## The trace problem, and this time it reaches zero

| Marketed as | INCI | % w/w | Actual |
|---|---|---|---|
| The calming peptide, named on the carton | **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4** | **0.0000001** | **1 ppb** |
| "Lactobacillus/Soymilk Ferment Filtrate" | same | **0.0000000** | **declared as zero** |
| Hyaluronic acid | Sodium Hyaluronate | 0.000001 | 10 ppb |
| "Rose stem cells" | Rosa Damascena Callus Culture Extract | 0.0003 | 3 ppm |
| "Grape stem cells" | Vitis Vinifera Callus Culture Extract | 0.0001 | 1 ppm |
| Soothing | Centella Asiatica Extract | 0.001 | 10 ppm |
| Soothing | Scutellaria Baicalensis Root Extract | 0.001 | 10 ppm |
| "Mannan" | Amorphophallus Konjac Root Extract | 0.01 | 100 ppm |

**The Lactobacillus/Soymilk Ferment Filtrate is declared at 0.0000000%.** It is
on the ingredient list and on our site's key-ingredient list, and the
manufacturer's own quantitative formula gives it a value of zero.

The carton sentence reads "MSC protects skin from UV A & B rays, and soothes the
irritated skin with calming ingredients — palmitoyl pentapeptide-4, centella
asiatica extract and scutellaria baicalensis root extract." Those three are at
1 ppb, 10 ppm and 10 ppm.

Also note the site's key-ingredient list says "Mannan", which is not an INCI
name and does not appear in the formula. The nearest thing is konjac root
extract at 100 ppm, konjac being a glucomannan source.

**What is actually at a working dose:** the four filters at 18.50%, butylene
glycol at 5.02%, dimethicone at 2.30% and glycerin at 1.00%.

## It is fragranced, and the pack says which allergens

Fragrance at 0.25%, with five EU-declared allergens on the carton:

- Benzyl Benzoate 0.025%
- Citronellol 0.0113%
- Hexyl Cinnamal 0.0113%
- Alpha-Isomethyl Ionone 0.0113%
- Limonene 0.0042%

The site record currently claims "Gentle Care — Suitable for sensitive skin and
daily use". A fragranced product with five declared allergens should not carry
an unqualified sensitive-skin claim. Soften it and name the fragrance so people
can decide.

**Documentation discrepancy, flagged not used:** the allergen sheet in the
dossier is dated 20 December 2019 and describes a lavender base containing
linalool at 21.7%, coumarin at 6.9% and geraniol at 4.9% — none of which appear
in the current formula's declared allergens. Either the fragrance was
reformulated after 2019 or the sheet belongs to a different version. **The
formula and the registered artwork agree with each other**, so those are the
authority and the 2019 sheet was not used for any figure on the page.

## What the carton says

- `[자외선 차단 기능성화장품]` — a **single-function** Korean cosmetic: UV
  protection only. Product 39 is registered for three functions. Clean, real
  difference between the two.
- 주성분 (declared actives): titanium dioxide, ethylhexyl methoxycinnamate,
  ethylhexyl salicylate, isoamyl p-methoxycinnamate. **Four**, confirming the count.
- "DERMATOLOGICALLY TESTED" and "Broad spectrum sun protection" are both printed.
- NET WT 40 g / 1.4 oz.
- The Russian panel adds "no parabens, alcohol or dyes", which checks out against
  the formula, and "reapply every 2 hours" in the sun.
- The Turkish panel adds "ideal under make-up".
- **No water-resistance claim anywhere**, and no test in the pack.
