## Two disclosure problems, in order of seriousness

### 1. The carton does not declare linalool, and the assessment says it must

The current valid safety assessment is explicit:

> Allergens from plant-derived raw materials … (An allergen is declared on the
> label when its concentration in the final preparation is >0.001%)
> **Lavandula Angustifolia (Lavender) Oil — Linalool: 0.0235%**

**0.0235% is 23 times the 0.001% declaration threshold.** The formula lists
linalool as a separate line item. **The printed carton INCI does not contain the
word linalool anywhere.** Every other GENOSYS product audited this week declares
it correctly, including both anti-wrinkle siblings.

This is a label compliance gap, not a marketing one, and it belongs with DTS MG
urgently. Our own page and record now disclose it regardless of what the carton
says.

### 2. It contains peanut oil, and we say so on the sibling but not on this one

`Arachis Hypogaea (Peanut) Oil` at **0.0087%**. The safety assessment addresses
it directly:

> It contains Arachis Hypogaea (Peanut) Oil and according to **ANNEX III/306**
> the manufacturer declares it contains **less than 0.5 ppm peanut proteins**.

The assessment also cites **SCCS/1526/14**, the opinion on peanut oil, which
treats it as a sensitisation risk governed by protein limits rather than by the
oil content.

Two things make this worth flagging:

- The peanut oil is not a deliberate addition. The assessment traces it to the
  **vitamin A palmitate raw material** — it is the carrier the retinyl palmitate
  arrives in.
- **Product 24, EyeCell, already carries "Contains peanut oil" on our site.**
  This product, applied over a far larger area of neck and chest, carried nothing.
  That inconsistency is ours, not the manufacturer's, and it is now fixed.

The refined oil is below the 0.5 ppm protein limit, so this is a disclosure
obligation rather than a reason not to sell it. But a peanut-allergic customer
must be able to find it before opening the box.

## Korea licenses this for wrinkles only, so the depigmentation claim has to go

The Korean carton panel reads `[주름개선 기능성화장품]` — a **wrinkle-improvement**
functional cosmetic, single function, with `주성분: 아데노신`, main ingredient
adenosine. There is no whitening function on the licence.

Our record claimed:

> It also has an excellent effect of depigmentation.

There is nothing in the formula to support it. No arbutin, no niacinamide, and
the only brightening-adjacent ingredient is ascorbyl glucoside at **0.025%**,
against roughly 2% in the efficacy literature. **Removed.**

## The claimed efficacy test does not exist, and the assessor said as much

Our record claimed "Efficacy test on improving wrinkles". No such report is on the
drive. More pointedly, the valid safety assessment raises it unprompted:

> **SUGGESTION** — All claims on the label regarding the efficacy of the product
> should be proved with supporting documents. **The phrases 'Anti-Wrinkle' need
> further documentation in order to be proven.**

…citing Regulation (EU) 655/2013 on claim justification. The carton nonetheless
prints **"Excellent Anti-Wrinkle Effect"**.

So the page says the defensible thing instead: **Korea licenses it for wrinkle
improvement on the basis of adenosine at 0.04%**, which is verifiable from the
carton and the certificate. The superlative and the phantom test are gone.

## The five peptides: one carries 97% of the load

| Peptide | Concentration | |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper Tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu)** | 0.005% | **50 ppm** |
| Palmitoyl Hexapeptide-12 | 0.0001% | 1 ppm |
| Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 (Argireline) | 0.000025% | **0.25 ppm** |
| Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 | 0.00002% | 0.2 ppm |
| **sh-Polypeptide-7** | 0.000001% | **0.01 ppm = 10 parts per billion** |

Roughly **51.5 ppm total, of which copper tripeptide-1 is 97%.** So of the five
peptides our record listed as equals, one is meaningfully present and four are
not. Argireline in the literature is studied at 5–10%; here it is a quarter of one
part per million.

The safety assessment describes sh-polypeptide-7 as a single-chain recombinant
human peptide produced by fermentation in *E. coli*, from a synthesised copy of
the human somatotropin gene. **Keep that off the customer page.** Naming a
recombinant growth-hormone peptide invites a drug claim, and at 10 ppb nothing
rests on it anyway. Name the ingredient, give the number, move on.

Also present: eight botanical extracts at **10 to 30 parts per billion**
(Scutellaria, Citrus junos, Camellia sinensis, Chamaecyparis obtusa water,
Houttuynia cordata, Artemisia princeps, Artemisia vulgaris, Lactobacillus ferment
lysate filtrate). Label dressing. And sodium hyaluronate at 0.001%, so the
"hyaluronic acid" in our claim list is **10 ppm**.

## A compliance action that was actually taken

The earlier assessment flagged retinyl palmitate:

> For the ingredient Retinyl Palmitate, Dr Baumann concluded that the maximum
> quantity on the final product must be for the face 0.05% and respectively for
> the body area … 0.025%. The percentage must be reduced to the above quantity.

The earlier formula table shows **0.0200%**; the current formula shows
**0.0111%**. It was reduced, and for a body-area product the ceiling is 0.025%, so
the current formula sits comfortably under. Worth recording as a case where the
assessment process worked.

The same earlier report suggested reducing copper tripeptide-1 "by a factor of
>10". **That suggestion does not appear in the current valid assessment**, which
concludes the product is safe with restrictions. Treat it as historical rather
than as a live finding, but it is worth one question to DTS MG.

## What the assessment does support

- Conclusion: "safe with restrictions for human health when used under normal or
  reasonably foreseeable conditions of use."
- **Patch test graded "Non Irritant" by QACS Ltd**, clearing "dermatologically
  tested" for the label, with the usual note that the volunteer count is not
  statistically significant.
- The carton's **"5 No-additions"**: no paraben, ethanol, artificial pigment,
  sulfate or artificial fragrance. The assessment confirms "no parfum present in
  the formula" — so **free of synthetic fragrance, but not fragrance-free**, since
  the lavender oil is there.

## Errors and gaps for `~/Desktop/genosys-artwork-corrections.html`

1. **★ Linalool 0.0235% is missing from the carton INCI** while the valid safety
   assessment names it as a required declaration. Compliance gap.
2. **The carton INCI is out of date.** It lists five ingredients not in the
   current formula — Hydrolyzed Hibiscus Esculentus Extract, Dextrin, **Palmitoyl
   Oligopeptide**, Sodium Polystyrene Sulfonate and Sorghum Bicolor Stalk Juice —
   and omits two that are in it, tocopherol and linalool. The carton therefore
   advertises a **sixth peptide the product no longer contains**.
3. **Superseded nomenclature:** the carton prints "Ceramide 3" for what the
   formula calls Ceramide NP.
4. **"Excellent Anti-Wrinkle Effect"** on the carton, against the assessor's note
   that the anti-wrinkle phrasing needs further documentation.
5. **Our depigmentation claim** contradicted the single-function Korean licence.
6. **Peanut oil is named in the INCI but absent from the carton precautions**,
   and was missing from our site entirely while the sibling product declares it.
7. **CPNP notification is dated 13 May 2015** and the formula has changed since
   (retinyl palmitate 0.02% → 0.0111%). Worth confirming the notification was
   updated.
8. **Only two product images exist**, `/images/ND.jpg` and
